Overview of the RECAL-Bench pipeline. Conversational videos from multiple publicly available datasets are processed to extract speaker–listener segments. Visual features are obtained through face cropping, facial analysis, and multiple 3DMM representations, while audio features are extracted from speech signals. These multimodal features, including facial action units, emotion features, 3DMM coefficients, transcripts, and acoustic descriptors, are used for training and evaluating listener facial expression generation models.

Abstract

Listener reaction generation aims to synthesize realistic facial behaviours of a listener conditioned on a speaker's audio-visual cues and the surrounding communication context. Recent benchmarking efforts have advanced this task by incorporating richer contextual signals and exploring diverse 3D facial representations and behaviour analysis pipelines. However, this heterogeneity spanning across diverse annotation schemes, reaction taxonomies, evaluation protocols, and rendering choices has significantly impedes fair comparison and reproducibility across datasets. As a result, it remains difficult to disentangle genuine methodological progress from dataset or protocol specific effects. A unified benchmark is therefore critically needed to enable systematic evaluation and accelerate progress in this domain. In this work, we introduce RECAL-Bench, a unified benchmarking framework for listener head generation and facial gesture modeling, constructed from multiple publicly available corpora using a consistent processing pipeline. RECAL-Bench emphasizes clearly defined speaker-listener roles, high-quality listener segments, and temporally aligned audio-visual streams. We provide unified annotations and features, standardized splits, and comprehensive evaluation protocols that assess reaction appropriateness, realism, and response diversity. Furthermore, we benchmark representative state-of-the-art models to establish strong baselines and expose open challenges. We believe RECAL-Bench will support the development of more natural, context-aware listener reaction generation systems.

Datasets

We introduce RECAL-Bench, a unified benchmark for listener facial expression generation. The benchmark is built upon multiple publicly available dyadic conversational datasets, including RealTalk, NoXi, MARS, NoXi-J, UDIVA, CCDb+, ViCo, and ViCo-X. These datasets capture face-to-face conversational interactions across diverse recording conditions, conversational settings, and languages, providing a broad range of speaker-listener dynamics, as shown in the table below. To ensure consistency and comparability across datasets, we apply a unified processing pipeline to all data. This pipeline systematically identifies speaker-listener roles, extracts standardized facial motion representations, and aligns multi-modal signals, resulting in a coherent benchmark tailored for listener modeling.

Evaluation Metrics

We evaluate listener facial expression generation across three complementary dimensions: appropriateness, speaker synchrony, and motion diversity.

Appropriateness

Measures how closely generated listener motions match ground-truth behaviors.

  • L2 Error — Motion-level discrepancy.
  • Fréchet Distance (FD) — Distributional similarity.

Speaker Synchrony

Measures the alignment between speaker behavior and generated listener responses.

  • Paired Fréchet Distance (P-FD) — Distributional similarity between speaker and listener features.

Motion Diversity

Measures the expressiveness and variability of generated listener behaviors.

  • Variation — Temporal dynamics within a sequence.
  • Diversity — Variability across generated responses.

Baselines

We benchmark RECAL-Bench against five representative listener reaction generation methods spanning recurrent, transformer-based, and diffusion-based approaches.

RLHG

Responsive Listening Head Generation uses speaker audio and identity-free 3DMM features with a listener reference frame to sequentially generate facial motion using an LSTM.

PCHG

Perceptual Conversation Head Generation generates listener motion from speaker audio and initial facial parameters using an LSTM-based framework.

L2L

Learning to Listen combines a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer with a transformer predictor to autoregressively generate future listener motion tokens.

ListenFormer

A transformer-based approach that uses audio-motion attention fusion and a transformer decoder conditioned on the listener's reference frame.

DiffListener

A non-autoregressive discrete diffusion model that combines speaker audio, facial, and textual cues to generate context-aware listener reactions.

Results

We evaluate five representative listener reaction generation methods across eight datasets and three 3D facial representations. Performance is measured using L2 error, Fréchet Distance (FD), Paired Fréchet Distance (P-FD), Variation, and Diversity on validation and test sets.

Cross-Setting Evaluation

We evaluate the generalization of listener reaction models across diverse conversational conditions by training on one dataset and evaluating on another. The cross-setting experiments are designed to isolate different sources of distribution shift: RealTalk → NoXi-J evaluates transfer from in-the-wild to lab conversations while also testing language generalization; NoXi-J → NoXi examines language and demographic shifts; CCDb+ → ViCo evaluates transfer from lab to in-the-wild conversations; and NoXi → NoXi (Unseen Languages) directly tests generalization to unseen languages.
